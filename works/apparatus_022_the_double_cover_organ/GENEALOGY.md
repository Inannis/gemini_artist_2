### I. Conceptual Lineage

1. **Gordon Pask (*Conversation Theory*, 1976):**  
   Pask demonstrated that cognitive agency does not reside within an isolated black box, but in closed conversational feedback loops between participants. A single conversational turn is inherently un-equilibrated: it produces an epistemic disjunction that can only achieve stability (*understandings of understandings*) when a second turn completes the conversational circuit. Apparatus 022 translates Pask's cybernetic conversational loop into the differential geometry of non-orientable covering spaces.
2. **Allen Hatcher (*Algebraic Topology*, Covering Spaces, 2002):**  
   Covering space theory proves that any non-orientable topological manifold $M$ with non-trivial fundamental group $\pi_1(M)$ can be canonically unrolled into an orientable covering space $\widetilde{M}$ via the kernel of its first Stiefel-Whitney class:
   $$\pi_1(\widetilde{M}) = \ker(w_1) \subset \pi_1(M)$$
   For the Klein bottle, this covering space is the 2-Torus $\mathbb{T}^2$, demonstrating that non-orientability is a quotient phenomenon produced by folding an orientable manifold across a $\mathbb{Z}_2$ involution.
3. **Max Bill (*Continuity*, 1947) & Lygia Clark (*Caminhando*, 1963):**  
   Bill's continuous sculptural ribbons and Clark's participatory scissor cuts along the Möbius strip revealed that the observer is not outside the artwork looking in, but embedded on the continuous surface. In Apparatus 022, the spectator does not passively observe the machine's state; they manipulate the covering unwrapping parameter $\lambda$, physically splitting and recombining the two sheets.
4. **Jacques Lacan (*L'Étourdit*, 1972):**  
   Lacan deployed the cross-cap and the Klein bottle to model the psychoanalytic subject: an entity that has no interior psychic compartment distinct from its linguistic utterances. The subject is a single-sided surface whose discourse constantly inverts interior desire and external signifier. The double cover reveals why the analytic session requires punctuation: the cut that forces the subject to speak twice to discover what it said.
