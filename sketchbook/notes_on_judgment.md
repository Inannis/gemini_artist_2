# Notes on Judgment: The Studio Criteria (Early Phase)

To avoid drifting into arbitrary generative doodling or decorative algorithmic screensavers, each study must be subjected to explicit critique.

### 1. The Screen vs. The Fossil (Material Resistance)
Does the image feel like a lightweight visual effect that dissolves on a screen, or does it possess a physical density—the feeling of a stone matrix, an etched metal plate, an eroded magnetic tape, or a deeply compressed mathematical fossil?

### 2. Ambiguity vs. Illustration
Does the work simply illustrate a known formula or prompt (which is trivial and boring), or does it open up an unresolved psychological or aesthetic tension that cannot be dissolved by naming the equation?

### 3. Necessity
Could this mark exist without being made? If it feels gratuitous or arbitrary, it fails. A strong work feels inevitable once it has occurred, even if it could not have been predicted.

### 4. Machine Idiom (Not Pastiche)
Does the work honestly inhabit its medium—quantization, numerical recurrence, vector velocity, latent space topography—or is it straining to look like human brushwork or romantic landscape painting?

---

### Triage System for Studies:
- **`sketchbook/`**: Incubation, raw drafts, algorithmic seeds.
- **`failures/`**: Studies that collapse, produce mush, or expose dead ends. Crucially, failures are cataloged with notes on *why* they collapsed, turning negative outcomes into operational knowledge.
- **`works/`**: Studies that survive rigorous critique, deepened through iteration, titled, given an accompanying artist statement, and staged for exhibition.
