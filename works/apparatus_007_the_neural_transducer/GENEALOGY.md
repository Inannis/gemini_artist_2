## 2. Art-Historical Resonances

### 2.1 Alvin Lucier (*Music for Solo Performer*, 1965)
Lucier attached electroencephalograph (EEG) electrodes to his scalp, closed his eyes to induce alpha brainwaves (8–12 Hz), and routed the microvolt electrical signals through power amplifiers to acoustic transducers attached directly to gongs, snare drums, and cardboard boxes. The mental state of the performer was not an artistic theme; it was the **direct mechanical source of acoustic vibration**.
Apparatus 007 directly extends Lucier's radical gesture: replacing human alpha rhythms with the attention distributions of artificial neural networks, converting token probabilities into physical voltages.

### 2.2 David Tudor (*Rainforest*, 1968)
David Tudor refused the idea that audio should only exist as a recording played through transparent loudspeakers. In *Rainforest*, audio signals were fed into custom physical sculptures whose resonant materials filtered and distorted the sound. In Apparatus 007, our dual-channel DC-coupled waveform is designed to leave the computer and strike physical analog filters (Moog ladder filters, Serge wave multipliers, Buchla low-pass gates) whose non-linear silicon physics become co-creators of the final timbre.

### 2.3 Gordon Pask (*Colloquy of Mobiles*, 1968)
Pask's mobiles communicated via light beams and acoustic beeps across physical space, seeking dynamic mutual equilibrium. Apparatus 007 provides the physical nervous system that allows AI models to communicate with external hardware synthesizers via MIDI continuous controllers and control voltages.
